The status column

Companion to Labels and status, which covers every other label in the product. Daniel’s reference: a status column of tinted capsules with outline icons (Passed, Failed, Running, Waiting on you, Parked), plus a full-row red tint on failures. His verdict was “generic”. Round two’s own mocks did the same. Here are four alternatives on the same 16 runs, in Daylight light and dark, that emphasise a failed or needs-you row without the row tint and capsule.

What D changes, as rules

  • No capsules in the status column. Status is a filled shape (dot, cross, diamond, arc, bars) plus a word only when the row isn’t passing. The shapes are distinct silhouettes, so they work in one colour and for colour-blind readers.
  • No row tints. A row earns emphasis by position (the “Needs attention” group at the top), words (a plain sentence saying what happened and what to do), and one action. Never by a fill behind the text.
  • Passing is quiet. Finished runs are ink: the trace shows ticks, with no colour and no word. Colour goes only to the failing step, the waiting step and the live arc.
  • The trace replaces the badge. One mark per step shows how far a run got and where it stopped, which a “Failed” pill can’t.
  • Phones keep the groups; each row becomes run name, sentence, and trace.
  • Accessibility: the trace has an aria-label (“step 4 of 6: failed”). Group headings are real rows with counts, so a screen reader hears “Needs attention, 2” before the rows.
