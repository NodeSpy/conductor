Labels and status

Daniel called the prototype’s tinted status pills generic. The same pill carries every label in the dashboard: run states, autonomy tiers, staged edits, trust, roles and plain facts. This page redesigns that whole vocabulary as one system, shown on five real screens in Daylight light and dark. Its companion, the status column, does the same for the status column in tables. Daniel picks from both pages together.

The vocabulary comes from the prototype itself: every UI.st, UI.tag and UI.tier label it renders, about 70 in all. The brief’s own list never arrived.

Recommendation: B, typed labels, with the status column’s D

These form one system. The state mark is the same everywhere, in the status column’s step trace, a bot header, a proposal or a pack row. Colour carries one meaning across the product: something needs a person, or something is live. Everything else is ink. Pills survive only as filters, because a pill now means “you can click this”.

Rules

  • Classify before you style. Every label is a state, a tier, a staged change, a trust level, a count, a fact or a role. Each job has one form. A new label picks its job; it never gets a new colour.
  • No static pills. A pill means clickable. Tinted fills behind words are gone, both from labels and from rows (see the status column).
  • Colour means attention. Ember for needs you, red for failed or refused, amber for waiting or risky, blue for live or pending, violet for shadow. Passed, installed, official, pinned and every fact are ink.
  • Words first. Tiers show their risk words (“Asks you first”), not their config names. Counts say “41 of 100 turns”, and staged edits say “not applied”.
  • One state per surface. A row or header shows one state mark. Further states are facts or go in the detail view.
  • Shapes carry state without colour, so labels read in one colour, for colour-blind readers and in forced-colours mode. Each mark has a text label for screen readers.
