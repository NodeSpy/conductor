A bot’s identity (18, 18b)

A display name (its id stays as it is), a tagline, a picture (one from the set, or an icon and a colour), and how it writes (Plain, Terse, Warm, Formal, plus style notes) with a live preview of a sample message. Picked in New bot (its own step, “Who it is”) and in the bot’s Settings. Without a picture, initials, as today. Config: avatar: {preset: hearth} or avatar: {icon: server, color: cocoa}, plus display_name, tagline, voice.

Where a bot’s colour goes

A · The picture and a thin rule

Good
Quiet: the colour is the picture’s background and a 3px rule on its messages, so a busy thread reads as one conversation with clear speakers.
Bad
The name itself stays ink, so you lean on the picture to tell bots apart at a glance.

B · The picture and a tint

Good
Most recognisable: each bot’s messages carry a faint tint of its colour.
Bad
Louder; with several bots in a team thread the page gets colourful, which works against “colour only for attention”.
My pick: A, recognisable and still calm.

Chat as a main destination (20)

Chat goes in the main navigation. Bots are people; teams are group chats (their members’ pictures stacked). Pinned and recent first, search for the rest (no full roster at 150). In a team chat the lead (or the team’s routing) picks up what you ask and answers or delegates; @mention a bot to ask it directly; “Ask everyone” sends to every member. Every reply says which bot it’s from, in its identity. An approval a bot raises is a compact card in the thread that opens the decision. A bot page’s Chat tab is this same conversation. On a phone the tab bar becomes Home · Needs you · Chat · Bots · More (Activity moves under More). The chat itself (tool calls folded, attachments, link previews) is item 19, built next.

1 · A messaging app: conversations on the left, the thread on the right

Good
Instantly familiar (Messages, Slack DMs); unread counts and who’s working show in the list; the phone is list → thread.
Bad
What the bot is doing lives one click away on its page.

2 · A rail of faces, the thread, and what the bot is doing beside it

Good
The bot’s context (its task, what waits for you, what it may do, what was shared) sits beside the chat, so you rarely leave.
Bad
A rail of faces stops scaling past a few dozen bots; less room for the thread on a laptop.
My pick: 1, with the context of 2 as a panel you can open from the thread header.
