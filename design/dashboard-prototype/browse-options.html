Browse options

Directions to choose from before anything is built: Bots and Workflows (one browsing pattern for both), the facts line under a list item, and the New bot flow with community bots. Each frame is the real shell and the prototype’s own components on fake data at the scale shown; open any frame full size from its caption. Every direction uses the one filter bar (search with qualifiers, then Status ▾ Team ▾ Folder ▾ menus, each searchable), which is already live on Activity and Needs you.

Bots

The page answers, in order: does any bot need me (or is something wrong); what are my bots doing; how do I get to a bot or a group. The duplicate list (every bot, then the tree with every bot again) goes in every direction. Folders are organisation only and teams are real groups with shared defaults: neither is an always-open tree.

Scale:

A · Exceptions first (your suggestion)

Good
Questions 1 and 2 answered at the top, and the page is the same size at 6 or 150 (needs you, then working, then “N other bots” as one line). Finding a bot is search plus Team ▾ and Folder ▾; teams are a line of links to their own pages.
Bad
An idle bot is only reachable by search or a filter; nothing shows how the fleet is organised at a glance.

B · Lanes, with groups as scopes

Good
Status and organisation in one view: a row of group tiles (all, each team or folder, with its own needs-you and working counts) scopes three capped lanes, Need you · Working · Idle. The groups row grows with the number of teams, not bots.
Bad
Busiest at 150: three lanes and a row of tiles compete; on a phone the lanes stack and Idle drops below the fold.

C · One status line, then groups that open

Good
Organisation first: each team or folder is one row with its roll-up (“2 need you · 3 working”), sorted so trouble is on top; open one at a time. Short at any scale (one row per group).
Bad
“What are my bots doing” takes a click per group; it is the tree again, only flatter and collapsed.
My pick: A, with one thing borrowed from B: when there’s more than one team or folder, the Team ▾ and Folder ▾ menus show each group’s needs-you count, so scoping to “work ▸ platform” is one pick and says what it holds. Teams keep their own pages (defaults and members).

Workflows

The same three questions, the same pattern as Bots so it is learned once: what’s broken or needs me, what’s running, how do I find one. Each row says what the workflow does in a sentence, then its health; where it lives and where it came from are filters (Source ▾, Folder ▾), not the second line. Workflows a bot generated for itself are on that bot’s page, not peers in this list (my call: they’re the bot’s machinery, and a person reaches them through the bot).

Scale:

A · Exceptions first

Good
Failing and not-running workflows first with the reason and one action, then running, then “N more, all healthy”. Constant size; the sentence says what each one does.
Bad
The healthy ones (most of them) are behind a click or a search.

B · Lanes, with sources as scopes

Good
All / Yours / each pack as scope tiles, three capped lanes below. Reads well when packs bring many workflows.
Bad
Lanes are cramped for a sentence per workflow on desktop and stack on a phone.

C · One status line, then groups that open

Good
Grouped by folder or pack with a health roll-up per group; trouble sorts to the top.
Bad
A failing workflow is one click deep inside its group.
My pick: A, the same as Bots: one pattern, learned once.

The facts line

The third line of a list item, redesigned instead of dropped: a few facts a person scans for (which bot or workflow, where, what kind of action and how risky, what it touches), each recognisable at a glance; IDs and hashes go to the detail page; links where they lead somewhere; production noticeable without being loud; no tinted pills. Real rows from Needs you and Activity.

Theme:

1 · Icon and value

Good
The most scannable: one icon per object type (bot, workflow, pull request, service, site, environment), values in quiet ink, links underlined; a risky action and production in bold, production with the warning mark and colour.
Bad
Icons must be learned; with five facts it gets long on a phone (it wraps).

2 · Labelled facts

Good
Nothing to learn: a tiny small-caps key over each value (Bot, Pull request, Environment, Does); production underlined in the warning colour.
Bad
Taller (two lines per fact) and closer to a form than a glance.

3 · A sentence of facts

Good
Reads as words: “platform-sre changes infrastructure on acme/infra ▲PRODUCTION aws_instance.web”, the objects in ink and linked, production in small caps with its mark. Most ownable in Daylight’s plain-words voice.
Bad
Least scannable down a column: the same fact sits at a different place on each row.
My pick: 1 (icon and value): it’s the one that scans down a list, the icons are the object icons used everywhere else, and it can go into the brand book as one component.

New bot

Leaving is obvious and safe in both: a visible close button, Esc, and a confirm only once something has been entered. The first step offers From a community bot (templates from installed packs and the official registry, with the same search and filters), From a starting point (the built-in shapes) and Blank. A community bot starts in practice mode, read-only; anything more is a visible grant signed with the passkey; its page says where it came from, and pack updates to its knowledge arrive in Needs you as an approval.

A · A large sheet over the Bots page

Good
You can see where you came from and the ✕ is where people look; a thin progress line replaces the big stepper (“Step 1 of 5 · How to start”). Full screen on a phone, ✕ top left.
Bad
Less room for the longer steps (permissions) on a small laptop.

B · Its own page, with a clear way out

Good
All the room; a sticky bar with “← Bots”, the steps as a quiet line of words and a Close button.
Bad
Still a takeover: the Bots page is gone while you’re in it.

The review step for a community bot (either direction)

What the template filled in: how it starts (practice mode, read-only), each thing it asks for as a grant you switch on, and what it comes with. A widening grant is signed with the passkey.

My pick: A (the sheet). The Packs page then lists which packs include bots, and a bot made from a template shows “from sre-bot 0.9.0” on its page.
